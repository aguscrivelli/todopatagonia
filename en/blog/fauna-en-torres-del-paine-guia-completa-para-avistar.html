Wildlife in Torres del Paine: a spotting guide

Torres del Paine is one of the best places in Patagonia to see wildlife, including pumas. Here are the species to look for and how to improve your chances.

📅 November 25, 2026 📍 Torres del Paine ⏱️ 5 min read

A park full of life

Torres del Paine is famous for its mountains, but it's also one of the best places in Patagonia to see wildlife. Its steppe, lakes and forests are home to species that are hard to find elsewhere, and many can be seen from the park's roads.

Guanacos: the park's hosts

It's almost impossible to visit Torres del Paine without seeing guanacos. They live in family groups on the steppe and are often seen grazing near the roads, especially around Laguna Amarga and Lake Sarmiento. In spring and summer the young, called chulengos, are born.

The puma

Torres del Paine is one of the places in the world where you have the best chance of seeing a wild puma. Even so, it's an elusive animal that moves mostly at dawn and dusk. Specialized tours with trackers greatly improve the odds. If you meet one on a hike, stay calm, don't run, don't turn your back and slowly move away.

Birds

  • Andean condor: seen soaring near peaks and cliffs.
  • Lesser rhea: the rhea of southern Patagonia, common on the steppe.
  • Upland goose: very common in meadows and along lakeshores.
  • Chilean flamingo: found in shallow lagoons.
  • Magellanic woodpecker: lives in the lenga forests; its drumming carries far.
  • Black-chested buzzard-eagle and caracaras: frequent raptors in the park's skies.

Other mammals

  • Culpeo and South American gray foxes: often seen, sometimes near campsites. Don't feed them.
  • Patagonian hog-nosed skunk: small and nocturnal, found on the steppe.
  • Huemul: the endangered South Andean deer lives in parts of the park. Sightings are rare.

Where and when

The steppe areas in the east of the park, such as Laguna Amarga and Lake Sarmiento, are best for guanacos, rheas and pumas. The forested valleys of the W trek are good for forest birds. Early morning and late afternoon are the most active times. Spring and summer are the liveliest seasons, though guanacos and many birds can be seen all year.

Responsible wildlife watching

  • Keep your distance: use binoculars or a telephoto lens instead of getting closer.
  • Never feed any animal: it habituates them to people and puts them at risk.
  • Drive slowly: animals cross the roads and collisions are a real threat.
  • Stay on the trails: it's a park rule and it protects wildlife.
  • Store food properly: at campsites, so you don't attract foxes or rodents.
  • Choose responsible operators: if you book a puma tour, ask how they work to avoid disturbing the animals.
