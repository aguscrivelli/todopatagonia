Bariloche in summer: what to do among lakes and mountains

In summer, Bariloche swaps snow for lakes, beaches and trails. Here are the must-do outings and how to plan your days.

📅 October 1, 2024 📍 Bariloche ⏱️ 6 min read

Bariloche beyond winter

San Carlos de Bariloche is known for snow, but summer may be its best season. Days are long, Lake Nahuel Huapi sparkles and the mountains fill up with trails, viewpoints and mountain huts. It's an ideal base for combining nature, food and relaxed outings.

Circuito Chico

The classic loop can be done by car, by bike or on a tour. It follows the shore of Nahuel Huapi to the Llao Llao Peninsula, with its historic hotel and San Eduardo chapel, and passes Punto Panorámico, one of the most photographed views in the area. By bike it's a lovely experience, but there are hills: take it easy.

Cerro Campanario

A chairlift takes you up in a few minutes to one of the best panoramic views in Bariloche: lakes, islands, the Llao Llao Peninsula and the mountains surrounding the city. You can also hike up on a short but steep trail.

Victoria Island and the Arrayanes Forest

Boat trips on Nahuel Huapi leave from Puerto Pañuelo and visit Victoria Island and the Quetrihué Peninsula, home to the Arrayanes Forest, a unique forest of cinnamon-colored trees. It's a full-day outing and highly recommended if you're traveling with kids.

Beaches and lakes

Yes, Bariloche has beaches. The water is cold, but on hot days the shores of Nahuel Huapi, Lake Gutiérrez and Lake Moreno get busy. They're great for kayaking or stand-up paddleboarding, or just for resting after a hike.

Hiking and mountain huts

Summer is hiking season. One of the most popular routes leads to Refugio Frey, on Cerro Catedral, surrounded by granite spires and a mountain lake. You can do it as a day hike or spend the night at the hut. There are many other trails of varying difficulty: check with the Club Andino or the national park information office before heading out.

Mount Tronador

A full-day trip takes you to the base of Tronador, the highest mountain in the area, on the border with Chile. From the Pampa Linda area you can see the Ventisquero Negro, a sediment-covered glacier, and hear the ice falls that give the mountain its name ("the thunderer").

Colonia Suiza and local food

Colonia Suiza, founded by immigrants in the early 20th century, is a good place to learn about the area's history and try regional cooking. In town, don't miss the chocolate, craft beer and dishes made with trout and venison.

Seven Lakes Road

If you have an extra day, the Seven Lakes Road links Villa La Angostura with San Martín de los Andes and is one of the most beautiful drives in the country. You can do it on a tour or by car, stopping at viewpoints and beaches.

Practical tips

  • Book early: January and February are high season, and hotels and tours fill up.
  • Protect yourself from the sun: mountain sun is strong, even when the temperature feels mild.
  • Bring a warm layer: nights and high areas are cool even in January.
  • Start early: in high season, viewpoints and parking lots fill up by mid-morning.
  • Look after the forest: fire risk is high in summer. Respect all fire bans.
