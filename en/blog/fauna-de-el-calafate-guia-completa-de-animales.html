El Calafate wildlife: a guide to animals and birds

Beyond glaciers, El Calafate is a great place to see Patagonian wildlife. Here is what lives there, where to find it and how to watch without disturbing it.

📅 November 25, 2026 📍 El Calafate ⏱️ 5 min read

Much more than glaciers

Most travelers come to El Calafate for Perito Moreno, but the town and its surroundings hide surprising wildlife. Between the steppe, Lake Argentino and the Andean forests live waterbirds, raptors and mammals adapted to wind and cold.

Laguna Nimez Reserve: birds steps from downtown

Just a few blocks from the center, the Laguna Nimez Nature Reserve is the best place to start. A signposted trail circles lagoons and wetlands where more than a hundred bird species can be seen throughout the year.

  • Chilean flamingo: the star of the show, with pink plumage over the water.
  • Black-necked swan: elegant and easy to recognize.
  • Upland geese: Patagonian geese usually seen in pairs on the grasslands.
  • Grebes and ducks: several species of diving birds and wild ducks.

The best times are early morning and late afternoon, when there's more activity and better light.

Raptors and steppe birds

On nearby roads and hills you will often see the Andean condor soaring on the wind, especially near rocky cliffs. The black-chested buzzard-eagle, the crested caracara and the chimango caracara are also common. The steppe is home to the lesser rhea (Darwin's rhea), which often crosses the road in small groups.

Mammals

  • Guanaco: the great symbol of the Patagonian steppe. Frequently seen on the roads to El Chaltén and Torres del Paine.
  • Culpeo and South American gray foxes: they come close to roads and sometimes to towns. Don't feed them.
  • Pichi: a small Patagonian armadillo, easier to spot in the warmer months.
  • Puma: it lives in the region, but it's elusive and sightings are very rare.
  • Huemul: the endangered South Andean deer lives in remote areas of the national park. Seeing one is a rare privilege.

On the lake and in the forest

On boat trips across Lake Argentino and on forest trails near the glacier you may see Magellanic woodpeckers, austral parakeets and various forest birds. The sound of the woodpecker drumming on lenga trunks often gives it away.

When to go

Spring and summer (October to March) are the best seasons for birdwatching, as many species arrive to breed. In winter there's less variety, but some species stay all year and the snowy landscape is beautiful.

Wildlife-watching tips

  • Bring binoculars: they make a huge difference, especially for birds.
  • Keep your distance: never chase or surround animals.
  • Don't feed them: it changes their behavior and harms their health.
  • Drive carefully: guanacos and rheas cross roads without warning.
  • Stay on the trails: in the reserve, this protects nests and vegetation.
  • Keep quiet: the calmer you are, the more you'll see.
