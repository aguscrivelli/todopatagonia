Fitz Roy trekking: a trail guide to El Chaltén

El Chaltén is Argentina's National Trekking Capital and the gateway to Mount Fitz Roy. Here are the trails to hike, how long they take and how to prepare.

📅 October 15, 2024 📍 El Chaltén ⏱️ 6 min read

The hiking town

El Chaltén is a young town at the foot of Mount Fitz Roy, in the northern sector of Los Glaciares National Park. What makes it special is that most trails start right from town: you don't need a car or a tour to reach some of the most famous landscapes in Patagonia.

Fitz Roy, which the Tehuelche people called Chaltén, rises above 3,400 meters. Together with Cerro Torre, it forms one of the most recognizable mountain skylines in the world.

Getting there

El Chaltén is about 220 kilometers from El Calafate, home to the nearest airport. There are daily buses between the two towns and the trip takes around three hours. You can also drive along Route 40.

The must-do trails

Mirador de los Cóndores and Mirador de las Águilas

A short hike that starts at the southern end of town. In less than an hour you reach a viewpoint over the valley, the town and, on clear days, Fitz Roy. Perfect for your arrival day or for sunset.

Laguna Torre

A full-day hike without major climbs that ends at Laguna Torre, with the glacier and Cerro Torre in the background. It's a good choice for your first long day: demanding because of the distance, not the elevation. Plan for six to eight hours round trip.

Laguna de los Tres

The great classic. The trail crosses forests and valleys to Poincenot camp and then tackles the final section: a very steep climb of about an hour that ends at the lake, right at the foot of Fitz Roy. It's a long day, around eight to ten hours in total. Start early, and if the last stretch gets too hard because of wind or fatigue, the view from Laguna Capri or the camp is already worth the hike.

Loma del Pliegue Tumbado

Less crowded than the others and with one of the most complete views in the area: Fitz Roy and Cerro Torre on the same horizon, plus Lake Viedma. The climb is steady and the final stretch is usually very windy.

When to go

The hiking season generally runs from October to April. Summer brings very long days and more services open, but also more people. In autumn, the lenga forests turn red and yellow. In winter, many hotels and shops close.

The weather is in charge

In El Chaltén the weather changes fast and the wind can be very strong. Fitz Roy spends many days hidden in clouds. That's why it pays to leave spare days in your itinerary and choose each morning's trail based on the forecast. If the day is clear, that's your Laguna de los Tres day.

Practical tips

  • Stop at the visitor center: at the entrance to town they give updates on trail conditions and park rules.
  • Wear broken-in footwear: these are long hikes, and blisters can ruin a trip.
  • Dress in layers: in a single day you can go from sunshine to icy wind.
  • Carry enough food: there are no services on the trails.
  • Leave no trace: pack out all your waste and stay on marked trails.
  • Watch the clock: plan to return in daylight, especially in autumn.
