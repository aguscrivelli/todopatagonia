Where to stay in Torres del Paine: complete guide

To visit Torres del Paine you can sleep inside the park, in refugios and campsites along the trek, or base yourself in Puerto Natales. We explain each option and how to book.

📅 November 25, 2026 📍 Torres del Paine ⏱️ 5 min read

Three ways to stay

Torres del Paine National Park, in Chile's Magallanes region, is huge and has few places to stay within its boundaries. So the first decision is where to sleep: inside the park, at the refugios and campsites along the trekking circuits, or in Puerto Natales, the nearest town.

1. Hotels inside the park

There are a handful of hotels and lodges inside the park or on its edges, with prime views of the Paine massif. Many offer full-board packages with guided excursions included. They are the most comfortable option and also the most expensive. Their big advantage: waking up facing the mountains and saving hours of transfers.

2. Refugios and campsites on the trek

If you're hiking the W or the O circuit, you'll sleep in refugios (mountain huts) or campsites along the way. Some are run by private concessionaires and others by CONAF, Chile's national forest service, which manages the park.

  • Refugios: beds in shared dorms, with meals available. Some offer private rooms.
  • Serviced campsites: bring your own tent or rent a pitched one, often with meals available.
  • Basic campsites: simpler sites with minimal facilities.

Very important: you need confirmed reservations to spend the night inside the park. Without a booking you can't camp or stay in refugios, and park rangers check. In high season, spots sell out months in advance.

3. A base in Puerto Natales

Puerto Natales, on the Última Esperanza fjord, is the gateway town for the park. It has the widest choice of hotels, guesthouses, apartments and restaurants, with a broader price range than inside the park. Scheduled buses and full-day tours to the park leave from there.

The downside is distance: getting to the park's main areas takes a couple of hours each way. If you're only doing day trips, it's the most convenient option.

What about El Calafate?

Many travelers combine Torres del Paine with El Calafate. There are full-day tours from El Calafate, but they are very long days, with several hours of driving and a border crossing. If you have time, it's better to cross to Puerto Natales and spend at least one or two nights on the Chilean side.

Which option suits you?

  • If you're hiking the W or O: refugios and campsites, booked well in advance.
  • If you want comfort and time inside the park: a hotel or lodge.
  • If you have few days or a tight budget: base yourself in Puerto Natales and do day trips.

Practical tips

  • Book lodging first: build your trekking itinerary around available refugios and campsites, not the other way around.
  • Buy your park entrance: there's an entrance fee, and it's best to arrange it before your trip.
  • Carry valid documents: if you're coming from Argentina, you'll cross an international border.
  • Be ready for wind: it's intense in the park. If you camp, bring a sturdy tent and good stakes.
  • Check the rules: fire use is heavily restricted. Always respect it.
