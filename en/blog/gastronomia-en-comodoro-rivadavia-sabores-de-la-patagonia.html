Food in Comodoro Rivadavia: flavors of Patagonia

Comodoro Rivadavia blends seafood with the tradition of Patagonian lamb and the heritage of its immigrants. Here's what to try in this city on the coast of Chubut.

📅 November 25, 2026 📍 Comodoro Rivadavia ⏱️ 5 min read

A city between the sea and the steppe

Comodoro Rivadavia, in southern Chubut on the San Jorge Gulf, is the largest city in central Patagonia. It was born and grew with oil, discovered in the area in the early 20th century, and welcomed workers from many backgrounds. That mix, together with the sea and the steppe, defines its cuisine.

San Jorge Gulf shrimp

The San Jorge Gulf is one of Argentina's main shrimp fishing grounds. In Comodoro it's eaten fresh and in many ways: grilled, in garlic, in casseroles, battered or as the star of pasta and rice dishes. If there's one dish to try in the city, this is it.

Fish and seafood

Hake and other South Atlantic fish appear on almost every menu, fried, baked or in stews. You can also find mussels, squid and, depending on the season, other shellfish from the Patagonian coast. Seafood casseroles are a good choice on cool, windy days, which are plentiful in Comodoro.

Patagonian lamb

The cooking of the plateau is here too. Patagonian lamb, raised on the steppe, is slow-roasted on a cross-shaped spit or grilled, and is the great classic of gatherings and weekends. It's served with salads, potatoes and often chimichurri.

The immigrant legacy

The oil industry brought families from different parts of Europe and from other Argentine provinces. That diversity shows in bakeries, homemade pasta, cured meats and family recipes. Chubut also has a strong Welsh tradition: tea with Welsh cakes is typical of the lower Chubut River valley, in the north of the province, and shows up in some tea houses in the region.

Sweets and regional flavors

Patagonian fruits such as calafate berries, rose hips and berries are turned into jams, liqueurs and ice cream. They make great souvenirs to take home.

Outings to pair with your meals

  • Rada Tilly: a neighboring beach town with a wide beach and a seaside promenade.
  • Cerro Chenque: the hill overlooking the city, with views of the gulf.
  • National Petroleum Museum: to understand the history that shaped the city.
  • Waterfront: great for a walk by the sea before or after eating.

Practical tips

  • Ask for the catch of the day: fresh products change with the season.
  • Adapt to local hours: as in most of Argentina, dinner is late.
  • Book on weekends: the best-known restaurants fill up.
  • Bring a jacket: the wind on the Patagonian coast is strong much of the year.
