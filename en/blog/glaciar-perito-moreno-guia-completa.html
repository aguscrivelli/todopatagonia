Perito Moreno Glacier: the complete visitor's guide

Perito Moreno is one of the most impressive and accessible glaciers in the world. This guide covers how to get there, what to do and how to make the most of your day.

📅 November 1, 2024 📍 El Calafate ⏱️ 6 min read

A glacier you can see face to face

Perito Moreno Glacier lies within Los Glaciares National Park, in Santa Cruz province, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. What makes it unique is how close you can get: from the walkways it is just a few hundred meters away, with an ice front about five kilometers wide rising several dozen meters above Lake Argentino.

It is also one of the few glaciers in the region that remains relatively stable, while most Patagonian glaciers are retreating.

Getting there from El Calafate

The glacier is about 80 kilometers from El Calafate, along a paved road that follows the lake. You have three options:

  • Organized tour: the most convenient. It includes transport, time on the walkways and, depending on the package, a boat trip.
  • Scheduled bus: buses leave from the El Calafate terminal and give you several hours at the glacier.
  • Your own or a rental car: full flexibility. The drive takes a little over an hour.

There is an entrance fee to the national park. Check the current rate before your trip.

The walkways: the best viewpoint

The walkway system has several kilometers of signposted trails, with circuits of different lengths and difficulty. Some sections have stairs, but there are accessible areas. From different balconies you can see the glacier front, the Brazo Rico and the Magallanes Peninsula.

The main show is calving: blocks of ice break off the front and crash into the water with a roar you can hear from far away. There is no schedule, so patience is key. Pick a balcony and stay a while.

Boat trips in front of the glacier

Boat trips approach the ice wall from the lake and give you a real sense of its height. They are short outings that are easy to combine with the walkways. Bring warm clothes: on deck, the wind coming off the ice is cold even in summer.

Walking on the ice

One of the most sought-after experiences is hiking on the glacier with crampons, always with licensed guides. There is a short walk suitable for most people in good physical shape, and a longer, more demanding one that goes deeper into the ice. Both have age limits and limited spots.

In high season they sell out weeks in advance, so if you want to do it, book as soon as you have your dates.

When to go

The park is open all year. Spring and summer (October to April) bring longer days and all excursions running. In winter there are fewer visitors and the scenery is beautiful, but some ice activities may not operate.

The ice bridge rupture

Every so often, the glacier's advance blocks the flow of water between Brazo Rico and the rest of the lake. The trapped water pushes against the ice until it carves a tunnel and the bridge eventually collapses. There is no fixed date and years can go by without it happening, but when it does, it draws visitors from all over the world.

Practical tips

  • Dress in layers: windbreaker, warm layer and a hat, even in summer.
  • Bring food and water: there is a food service on site, but having your own gives you flexibility.
  • Plan a full day: between the drive, the walkways and a boat trip, time flies.
  • Stay on the trails: don't approach the shore outside authorized areas. Falling ice creates dangerous waves.
  • Protect yourself from the sun: the glare off the ice is intense. Sunglasses and sunscreen, always.
