Bariloche: Patagonia's Alpine Gem in Argentina

Where the Andes meet pristine lakes in Argentina's most captivating mountain town.

Why Visit

🏔️
Dramatic Alpine Scenery

Bariloche is ringed by snow-capped peaks and crystal-clear lakes that rival the Swiss Alps. The landscape of jagged mountains reflected in turquoise waters creates some of South America's most breathtaking vistas.

🥾
World-Class Hiking & Trekking

From day hikes to multi-day treks, Bariloche offers trails for every level through pristine Patagonian forests and alpine terrain. The nearby Circuito Chico loop and Fitz Roy region provide unforgettable wilderness experiences.

🍫
Chocolate Capital of Argentina

Bariloche's chocolate shops are legendary, crafting artisanal Swiss-style chocolates since the early 20th century. Strolling Mitre Avenue sampling truffles and dulce de leche delights is an essential experience.

❄️
Adventure Year-Round

Winter transforms Bariloche into Argentina's premier ski destination with Cerro Campanario and Cerro Otto resorts. Summer brings perfect hiking weather, while spring and autumn offer ideal conditions with fewer crowds.

What to Do

Cerro Campanario Chairlift & Viewpoint

Ride the iconic cable car to this 1,050-meter peak for 360-degree panoramic views of Bariloche, its lakes, and surrounding mountains. On clear days, you can see all the way to the Andes. The viewpoint has a restaurant and is accessible year-round.

Circuito Chico: Scenic Loop Drive

This stunning 80-kilometer route winds through Patagonian forests, past turquoise lakes, and alongside dramatic peaks. Stop at viewpoints, hike short trails, visit Puerto Blest, and explore charming villages like Villa Mascardi along the way.

Trekking to Fitz Roy & Laguna de los Tres Picos

Embark on challenging but rewarding day hikes near El Chaltén (3 hours from Bariloche) to reach these iconic granite peaks. The views of Mount Fitz Roy and surrounding turquoise lagoons are among Patagonia's most photographed vistas.

Chocolate Tours & Artisan Shops

Explore historic chocolate factories and boutiques along Mitre Avenue and in the Centro Cívico district. Many offer tastings and demonstrations of how Swiss-influenced Bariloche chocolate is crafted using traditional methods.

Kayaking & Water Sports on the Lakes

Paddle across pristine Lago Nahuel Huapi or take guided kayak tours to remote coves and beaches. Summer also offers windsurfing, fishing expeditions, and boat cruises that showcase the region's stunning lakeside landscapes.

Winter Skiing at Cerro Campanario & Cerro Otto

During Argentina's ski season (June–August), Bariloche's two resorts offer varied terrain for all abilities. Cerro Campanario is closer to town and family-friendly, while Cerro Otto offers more challenging runs and cross-country skiing.

How to Get There

By Air: Bariloche's Aeropuerto Internacional Teniente Luis Candelaria is well-connected to Buenos Aires (2–2.5 hours, multiple daily flights) and other major Argentine cities. Direct flights from Chile are also available. Major carriers include Aerolineas Argentinas, LATAM, and low-cost options like Flybondi.

By Bus: Long-distance buses connect Bariloche to Buenos Aires (20+ hours), Mendoza (16 hours), and other cities across Argentina and Chile. Overnight buses are comfortable with reclining seats and are a budget-friendly option. The bus terminal is centrally located.

Distances: Bariloche is approximately 1,600 kilometers (1,000 miles) south of Buenos Aires, 800 kilometers from Mendoza, and 350 kilometers from Puerto Varas, Chile. For scenic drives, allow extra time to appreciate the landscape.

Where to Stay

💰 Budget

Hostel options like Refugio Patagonia and Selina offer dormitory beds and private rooms with communal kitchens and social atmospheres perfect for meeting other travelers. Budget hotels and family-run cabañas in nearby neighborhoods like Llao Llao provide affordable, authentic stays with mountain views and local hospitality.

🏨 Mid-range

Three- and four-star hotels like Hotel Edelweiss and Fierro Hotel offer comfortable rooms with lake or mountain views, on-site restaurants, and convenient locations near the town center. Many mid-range properties feature spa facilities, good breakfast spreads, and helpful concierge services for activity planning.

⭐ Luxury

Five-star resorts like Llao Llao Hotel and Resort and Ausangate offer unparalleled luxury perched on the lake peninsula with panoramic views, gourmet dining, and world-class amenities. These properties provide premium experiences including spa treatments, fine wines, and personalized adventure concierge services.

Practical Tips

📅
Best Time to Visit

December–February (summer) offers warm weather, long days, and perfect hiking conditions; expect crowds and higher prices. June–August brings world-class skiing, while September–November (spring) and March–May (autumn) offer fewer tourists, mild weather, and stunning wildflower displays.

🌡️
Weather & What to Pack

Bariloche's weather is highly changeable—bring layers including a waterproof jacket, warm fleece, and sturdy hiking boots. Even in summer, temperatures drop in the evening. Winter requires heavy winter gear, thermal layers, and waterproof outerwear.

💱
Currency & Costs

Argentina uses the Argentine Peso (ARS). While Bariloche is pricier than other Argentine destinations, it remains affordable compared to international standards. ATMs are abundant on Mitre Avenue; most establishments accept both cash and credit cards.

🗺️
Getting Around Town

Bariloche's center is compact and walkable. For the Circuito Chico and remote hikes, rent a car or book guided tours through hotels or tourism offices. Local buses serve outlying neighborhoods. Taxis and rideshare apps are reliable for evening transport.

🎫
Book Activities in Advance

During peak season (December–February and July–August), popular treks, guided tours, and accommodations fill quickly. Reserve ski passes, tour operators, and lodging weeks ahead to secure your preferred dates and prices.
